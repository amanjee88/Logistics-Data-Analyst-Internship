This report presents a structured strategic plan for analysing last-mile delivery performance for a fictional e-commerce logistics operator serving an urban market in India. The project focuses on improving delivery reliability, understanding delivery-time variation, monitoring transportation cost, and identifying operational bottlenecks through a repeatable Python-based analytics workflow. The scenario is proposed for learning and planning purposes; it does not describe a real company or use confidential operational records.

The report defines the business problem, objectives, scope, stakeholders, and five key performance indicators: on-time delivery rate, average delivery time relative to the promised time, average delay among late deliveries, transportation cost per shipment, and first-attempt delivery success rate. For every KPI, it explains the purpose and the calculation or measurement rule so that later analysis uses consistent definitions. The plan also identifies relevant variables such as order date, promised and actual delivery timestamps, region, distance, shipment weight, vehicle type, transportation cost, and first-attempt outcome.

The research section uses public sources to frame the importance of reliable logistics, describe potential transport-context sources, and identify a recognized approach to route optimization. These sources provide context and technical guidance; they do not supply order-level data for the fictional operator. The report therefore distinguishes public contextual references from any future shipment-level dataset. If a small example is used to test the accompanying starter Python code, it is explicitly labelled synthetic and must not be interpreted as evidence of real logistics performance.

The strategic roadmap covers data sourcing and documentation, schema validation, cleaning, exploratory data analysis, KPI reporting, predictive modelling, and later optimization. A Python starter script demonstrates schema checks, KPI calculation logic, and basic data-quality checks. The proposed follow-up stages may explore regression for delivery-time estimation, segment analysis to identify recurring bottlenecks, and route optimization where reliable distance matrices and operational constraints are available. Model results and recommendations will be validated before being used for any real operational decision. The overall aim is to establish a feasible, transparent foundation for subsequent logistics analysis tasks.
